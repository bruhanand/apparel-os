## 1. What this document fixes

- How a person signs in and keeps a session (`PRD-SEC-001`), and how a service identity is authenticated by its credential (`PRD-SEC-018`; 2.3).
- How access is granted and checked: permissions, roles, role assignments, scope, restricted fields, and the PostgreSQL scope controls behind them (`PRD-ACS-001`–`PRD-ACS-008`, `PRD-SEC-005`).
- How approvals work: rules, limits, requests, decisions, independence, stand-ins, bulk and phone approval, and the approval of a document that a job posts later (DEC-097).
- The one inbox, My work, and the exception record (`PRD-ACS-009`, `PRD-EXC-001`).
- The tables of `access`, `inbox` and `exceptions`, by name, key and constraint (section 13).

It fixes no person, role holder, scope, limit, allowlist, reason, owner or due time. Those are KDPS's under policy 2 and stay OPEN until configured (V-01 to V-04; `POL-02.10`, `POL-02.11`). Module-map 4.3, 4.8 and 4.13 name the operations; this document makes them concrete. Labels (**Design choice**, **Proposed**, **OPEN**) mean what module-map section 1 says.

## 2. Who acts

### 2.1 Users

- A user is one person's login in one Organisation, with one My work. A person who serves several Organisations holds a separate user in each; no login, session or role assignment crosses Organisations (`PRD-ACS-020`, DEC-093).
- A user has a UUIDv7 identifier and a login identifier unique in the Organisation, compared without regard to letter case. A login identifier is never given to another person, even after the user ends. **Design choice.**
- A user is Active or Disabled, and may be Ended; it is never deleted, so its history stays (`PRD-ACS-013`). Creating a user after the setup step, which creates the first two Active (9.11), and every change to a user's details, personas held or state, is a user version: a draft, then Awaiting approval, then decided by an authorised person other than the preparer (9.11, DEC-112). A user whose first version is not yet approved has no state in force and cannot sign in (7.1 step 1). Disabling, or ending, takes effect at once when it is approved: in the decision's transaction, no new sign-in is allowed and every session of the user is revoked, from the moment of the decision, not from the next business day (`PRD-SEC-008`, `PRD-SEC-019`; product owner, 7 Oct 2026, DEC-118, RR-321). How the version is dated so that this holds is in 9.5. The state names are the baseline of DEC-105 (DM-4; [design-language.md](../ui/design-language.md) section 7).
- A user holds personas, with dates. A persona sets home screens, menus and summaries and grants nothing (`PRD-ACS-002`, `PRD-ACS-003`; [personas.md](personas.md) section 1).
- Customers and suppliers are records, not users ([personas.md](personas.md) section 4).

### 2.2 Partner users

- Partner staff are users who hold Store personas on their authorised Stores only. Their statement and ledger access comes from role assignments, not from a separate persona (`PRD-FRN-007`, DEC-042).
- A partner user's record names the partner it works for. Every role assignment of a partner user selects Stores, never all members and never a Site, and each selected Store must have that partner among its partner associations on the assignment's dates ([structure-and-masters.md](../masters/structure-and-masters.md) 3.1). Maintain refuses a wider scope (`PRD-FRN-001`, `PRD-FRN-007`). **Design choice.**
- Partner users arrive with the `partners` module in stage 5. The rule is fixed now so the stage 1 tables allow it. What partners see in their statements and ledgers is OPEN (`POL-12.04`; KDPS; stage 5).

### 2.3 Service identities

- A service identity is a non-human actor with its own audit identity, scoped credentials and least-privilege access. It has no screens (`PRD-SEC-018`).
- Two kinds. **Design choice.**
  - An internal identity, under which the worker runs a job or delivers the outbox (module-map section 10).
  - An outside caller with its own credential, such as an authenticated adapter callback (`PRD-INT-007`). The local helper and the Tally local gateway are this kind too: they call the server over HTTPS with a service-identity credential, and nothing calls into a Store or office (DEC-105; [deployment.md](../platform/deployment.md) section 6, D-6). The secret is shown once, stored only as an Argon2 hash, revocable and never logged (`PRD-SEC-014`).
- It gets access only through role assignments, scoped as narrowly as its work needs (`PRD-SEC-018`).
- Some record types are service-only: `access.effective_grant`, `audit.audit_seal`, `audit.audit_partition`, `kernel.outbox_event` and `inbox.work_item` are held by service identities only, never by a person's role. The registry marks them; the role editor leaves them out and a role change that holds one is refused, and so is assigning such a role to a person (`access.service-only-permission`; `PRD-SEC-018`; `S1-F01-T29`). **Design choice** (product owner, 7 Oct 2026).
- It never decides an approval: an approver is an authorised person (`PRD-ACS-006`).
- When a job carries out a person's decision, the audit record names the service identity as the actor and the person on whose behalf it acts (section 9.8). **Design choice.**

### 2.4 Devices

- A device is registered online by an authorised person and has a device code unique in the Organisation (`PRD-OFF-002`). `access` keeps its identity and state; `pos` keeps its billing facts; `hr` uses registered devices for attendance (`PRD-HRM-004`; module-map 2.3).
- A lost device is Revoked (`PRD-SEC-008`, DEC-092). Revoking ends its sessions at their next request. Revoked is final; a replacement is a new device.
- A cloned or restored device cannot continue the identity (`PRD-OFF-010`). How a device proves its identity is the offline counter design (GC-8).
- Offline delegation has a bounded expiry instead of instant revocation (`PRD-SEC-008`, `PRD-OFF-003`).

## 3. Sign-in and sessions

### 3.1 Sign-in

1. The person gives the Organisation code, the login and the password. The directory finds the Organisation's database by its code; it holds nothing else (DEC-093; [deployment.md](../platform/deployment.md) sections 3 and 4).
2. The password is checked against its Argon2 hash (PRD Stack: Authentication).
3. The second factor is checked: the code from an authenticator app (TOTP) (`PRD-SEC-001`, `POL-02.17`). Nothing is sent by message before stage 5, and sign-in needs nothing sent (DEC-099).
4. A session is created in the Organisation's database, and its identifier goes in the cookie ([deployment.md](../platform/deployment.md) section 3).

- One refusal message covers a wrong Organisation code, login, password or code, so a failed attempt does not say which part was wrong. **Design choice.**
- Every attempt in a known Organisation writes an access record ([numbering-and-audit.md](../platform/numbering-and-audit.md) 5.2; `PRD-SEC-007`). An attempt with an unknown Organisation code goes only to the service log, without what was typed (`PRD-SEC-014`). **Design choice.**
- Repeated failures slow or stop further attempts. Throttling counts attempts by the typed login and by the source address. The answers for a login that exists and for one that does not are equivalent: the same refusal and the same slowing, so throttling never shows which logins exist (product owner, 6 Oct 2026, DEC-116; RR-209). The thresholds, and the password rules, are OPEN (GC3-5).
- **How throttling works** (`S1-F01-T08`). **Design choice.** The throttling setting is an effective-dated setting of `access` with two values and no default: a failure limit and a window. Once the failed sign-ins of the typed login, or of the source address, within the window reach the limit, a further attempt is answered "slowed" (`access.sign-in-slowed`) without any credential being checked, until older failures leave the window; a slowed attempt adds no failure, and still writes its access record. A failure is counted in `access.sign_in_failure` by a keyed digest of the typed login, made under the Organisation's key (section 6), so a typed login that matched no user is never kept in a readable form (`PRD-SEC-014`). Every attempt that is not slowed costs one Argon2 check, against a hash no one knows when the login matched no user who may sign in, so a login that exists and one that does not also take about as long. While the throttling setting, or the Organisation's timezone that dates it, is not set, sign-in is unavailable and names the missing setting (code-house-rules 12.14); `dev` and tests hold synthetic values.
- **A missing security setting blocks sign-in** (product owner, 7 Oct 2026, DEC-118). Sign-in stays unavailable, naming what is missing, while any required security setting is not set: the Organisation's timezone, the sign-in throttling (3.1), the password rules (3.2) and the office session limits (3.3). Nothing falls back silently to a value. The setup step validates that every one of them is present and refuses a request without them (9.11). **As built** (`S1-F01-T25`): sign-in checks all four in one read and names every one not set, in the order above, in the refusal's `missing` (`access.sign-in-unavailable`).
- Production and `kdps-test` require the authenticator code. Development uses the same sign-in as production, with synthetic users, so it has no easier path and nothing in it weakens production login (`POL-02.17`, `PRD-ACS-017`; DEC-105, [deployment.md](../platform/deployment.md) D-6). **Design choice.**
- **The authenticator code as built** (`S1-F01-T08`). **Design choice.** TOTP of RFC 6238 with the parameters every authenticator app reads by default: HMAC-SHA-1, six digits and a 30-second step, with a 160-bit secret (RFC 4226 section 4). A code of the current step or of one step either side is accepted, for a phone clock a little off: RFC 6238 section 5.2 recommends allowing at most one time step of delay and resynchronising beyond it. These are technical values of the standard, not a KDPS value or a policy tolerance, so no setting holds them. A code is never accepted for a step already used, so a code seen once cannot be replayed (3.3).

### 3.2 Passwords and the second factor

- At the first sign-in the user enrols an authenticator app and confirms it with a code. The secret is encrypted at rest (section 6) and never shown again. **Design choice.**
- **The QR code at enrolment** (product owner, 7 Oct 2026, DEC-118; RR-280). Beside the setup key, the enrolment screen shows the setup link (`otpauth:`) as a QR code for the phone to scan. The web app draws it in the browser with the maintained QR code library the PRD Stack names (Authentication), from the answer that shows the secret once; the secret never goes to an outside QR service or any other origin, and the QR image is never stored, cached or logged (`PRD-SEC-014`; code-house-rules 12.1, 12.6). The setup key in groups of four stays as the manual fallback ([design-language.md](../ui/design-language.md) 10.20).
- A user changes their own password after giving a fresh authenticator code. **Design choice.**
- **A new user's first password.** Every user starts with a temporary password, entered by whoever creates the user: the platform operator, for the setup step's first Admin and first approver (9.11), who types it at the operator command's prompt, never in its arguments; for any later user, the person creating it, which needs create on users (9.11). It is checked against the password rules like any other password (GC3-5), stored only as an Argon2 hash marked temporary, with the user's draft version, and usable only once that version is approved (2.1), and never shown again, logged, put in an error or copied into an audit record (`PRD-SEC-006`, `PRD-SEC-014`). Nothing is sent before stage 5 (DEC-099): the person who entered it hands it over in person. **Design choice.**
- At the first sign-in the user gives the Organisation code, the login and the temporary password, enrols an authenticator app, then sets a new password after a fresh code. Until both are done, the sign-in reaches nothing else: no record, no My work and no role assignment (7.1 step 1). After a reset, the user first does what the reset requires: a new password after a password reset, enrolment after an authenticator reset, and both after a reset of both; until then, likewise, nothing else. **Design choice.** Whether a temporary password expires unused, and when, is a password rule and stays OPEN with the others (GC3-5); until it is set, nothing expires it, and it stays valid until the user replaces it, a reset replaces it, or the user is disabled.
- **Where a temporary password lives** (RR-213, settled by `S1-F01-T08`). **Design choice.** In `password_credential`, which holds one current row per user and names the user version the password was entered with. It signs in only while the user has an Approved, Active version in force (2.1), so a pending user's never does. A credential that is replaced, by the password change or a reset, keeps no hash at all; the decision that rejects a user's first version retires that user's credential the same way, so no form of a password outlives its use.
- **The password rules** (`S1-F01-T08`). **Design choice** of shape; the values are OPEN (GC3-5): an effective-dated setting of `access` holding the least number of characters, counted as characters, not bytes. While no rules are in force, no password can be set (`access.password-rules-not-set`, code-house-rules 12.14). A refused password (`access.password-refused`) names the rule it failed, in a `missing` item `password-rule` with the rule (`minimum-length`) and the setting's value, never the password, so the screen says what the password needs (`S1-F01-T31`).
- Whoever entered a temporary password knows it until the user replaces it. Enrolment and the password change each write an access record with its time and network address ([numbering-and-audit.md](../platform/numbering-and-audit.md) 5.1, 5.2), so a first sign-in by someone else shows in the history, and a user who finds their login already enrolled reports it for a reset. **Design choice.**
- **A repeated request that carries a secret.** Neither the hash an idempotency record keeps nor a refused request kept for investigation holds a secret or any form of one: the hash covers the non-secret fields only (`PRD-INT-002`, `PRD-SEC-014`). **Design choice.** How a replay of such a request is told identical from changed content (creating a user, a reset, a password change, or any request carrying an authenticator code) is fixed by [code-house-rules.md](../platform/code-house-rules.md) 12.5 and 12.6 (RR-207, DEC-113). The setup step has its own rule (9.11).
- A lost authenticator or a forgotten password is reset by a person who holds edit on the user's credentials, never by the user alone. The user enrols again or sets a new password at the next sign-in, and every session of the user is revoked. Before stage 5 nothing is sent (DEC-099), so a temporary password is handed over in person, as for a first password (above). **Design choice.** Each reset writes an access record.
- A reset needs no second approver. It is a protected action (3.3), so the person who resets gives a fresh authenticator code, and nobody resets their own credential, even if they hold edit on all credentials (DEC-105, GC3-4). **Design choice.**
- **Recovering a first user** (product owner, 6 Oct 2026, DEC-116; GC3-13, RR-206). While no user of the Organisation holds the permission to reset another user's credential, a lost password or authenticator of the first Admin or the first approver (9.11) is restored by the platform operator with a recovery command: an operator command like the setup step, never an API route. It verifies the person's identity and records how it was verified, revokes every session of the user (`PRD-SEC-008`), writes an access record ([numbering-and-audit.md](../platform/numbering-and-audit.md) 5.1), and grants no role or permission. The user then completes it at the next sign-in, as after any other reset. Once a user of the Organisation holds the reset permission, the command is refused, and resets follow the bullets above. **As built** (`S1-F01-T10`). **Design choice.** The operator names the Organisation code, the user's login and what to reset (the password, the authenticator or both), and types at the prompt how the identity was verified and, for a password, the new temporary password, checked against the password rules. The command acts under the `setup` service identity, the platform operator's own (9.11); a user is a first user when the setup record names them; "holds the reset permission" means an assignment in force today granting edit on `access.user_credential` to any user, whatever the user's state. It writes an audit record of the reset, an `operator-recovery` access record holding the words of the verification, and the session revocations of 3.3.
- Signing in, changing one's own password or second factor, and ending one's own sessions are part of authentication (`PRD-SEC-001`), not access to a record (`PRD-INT-001`), so they need no role assignment. They act only on the signed-in user's own credentials and sessions, grant nothing and reach no other record. **Design choice.**

### 3.3 Sessions

- A session is a row in the Organisation's database: the user, the device where registered, its kind, its start, its last activity and its state. The cookie holds the Organisation code and a random identifier of at least 256 bits from a cryptographically secure generator; the database keeps only the identifier's SHA-256 hash, which suffices for a random value of that length where a password needs Argon2. **Design choice.**
- **Finding the Organisation of a request** (DEC-093, `PRD-ACS-020`). At sign-in the database is found from the code the person types (3.1). Every later request finds it from the cookie: the server reads the Organisation code from it, finds that Organisation's database in the directory, then looks for the session there by the hash of the identifier; Authenticate runs only then (7.1 step 1). The directory keeps no session, user or identifier hash, and no request searches more than one Organisation's database. The code is no secret: the person typed it, and it is the one routing fact the directory already holds. A cookie whose code the directory does not list, or whose identifier matches no session in that database, counts as not signed in: the request gets the same answer as any request without a session, and the service log notes it without the cookie's value (`PRD-SEC-014`). Changing the code in a cookie reaches no other Organisation's session, since a session exists only in its own Organisation's database. **Design choice.**
- The code and the identifier travel in one host-only cookie of one name ([deployment.md](../platform/deployment.md) section 3), encoded so that neither can be mistaken for the other, so signing in to another Organisation replaces the cookie and ends nothing in the first Organisation's database. **Design choice.** The name is `__Host-aos-session`: the `__Host-` prefix makes a browser refuse the cookie unless it is `Secure`, host-only and on path `/` (`S1-F01-T08`).
- Kind: a session on a registered billing device is a shared POS session; any other is an office session. Policy 2 names these two kinds (`POL-02.18`). **Design choice** of how the kind is decided.
- The idle-lock and absolute limits per kind are effective-dated settings in `access` (`PRD-ACS-017`, `PRD-MOD-010`). Policy 2 states 5 minutes for shared POS sessions, 15 minutes for office sessions and 12 hours absolute (`POL-02.18`). They apply once policy 2 is Signed and the Admin has validated them (V-04), as DM-6 requires: a person holding the validate permission who did not enter them (DEC-105). Until then no value is active on production; synthetic tests use labelled synthetic values. On `kdps-test`, before policy 2 is Signed, sessions use the values `POL-02.18` states, as settings of the test setup that enable no other gated action (DEC-102, DEC-071).
- When the idle limit passes, the session locks and the screen keeps the unfinished work. The same user unlocks it with their password. Someone else at the same device signs in with a session of their own. **Design choice.** The screen also shows the lock on its own timer, from the idle limit in the session read, without waiting for a request and without polling (product owner, 7 Oct 2026, `S1-F01-T30`; RR-301); the server stays the check and locks the session at the next request.
- When the absolute limit passes, the session ends. Unfinished work is kept and offered back after the next sign-in: drafts already saved stay as Draft records; unsaved screen input is kept on the device for the same user and Organisation (`PRD-ACS-017`, `POL-02.18`, `PRD-UXP-003`). Restricted fields are never kept in that copy; the person enters them again (`PRD-SEC-006`). **Design choice.**
- A session, every session of a user, or every session of a device can be revoked. Revocation takes effect at the next request and closes the session's live-update stream (`PRD-SEC-008`). Event: `access.session-revoked`.
- Protected actions ask for a fresh authenticator code (`PRD-SEC-001`): deciding an approval, changing access, changing bank details, showing or exporting an encrypted field, resetting another user's credential, and changing one's own password (3.2). The first four are the baseline of GC3-6 (DEC-105); the reset follows from GC3-4, and the own-password change is a **Design choice**. How long a code stays fresh is a setting with no default; it stays OPEN (GC3-6; KDPS Owner; stage 1 live use). Until it is set, each protected action asks for a new code. **Design choice** (fail-safe; no duration chosen).
- **Changing an essential security setting after setup** (product owner, 7 Oct 2026, DEC-118; RR-334). The sign-in throttling (3.1), the password rules (3.2) and the office session limits (3.3) are changed after setup only as a prepared new version of the setting, approved by an authorised person other than its preparer, effective-dated and kept with its history like any change to access (9.11; `POL-02.06`, `POL-02.07`). Preparing needs edit and deciding needs approve on the setting's record type; deciding is a protected action (3.3) and writes an audit record and a permission-change access record. The new version may take effect from the moment of its decision, dated like a user version (9.5), or from a later date; it never starts in the past. Removing a setting is not a change: a required setting cannot be left unset once set. A version set for live use is still validated by a person who did not enter it (V-04, DM-6) before it counts as KDPS's value (code-house-rules 12.14). The general settings screen waits for `S1-F04`; until then this change has its own `access` routes and a small form built from existing parts ([design-language.md](../ui/design-language.md) 10.19). **Design choice** of the route and the form.
  - **As built** (`S1-F01-T25`). **Design choice** throughout; no value is chosen. The record type is `access.setting`, with view, edit and approve: edit prepares, approve decides, and nothing creates or removes a setting through a permission. The routes are `listSecuritySettings` (GET `/api/access/security-settings`, view: each of the three settings with every version, newest first, and the one in force) and `prepareSecuritySettingVersion` (POST `/api/access/security-settings/versions`, edit: the setting, every value, the origin and when it takes effect, all stated, none defaulted); the decision is the ordinary Decide route, action type `access.setting.change`, approved independently with a listed reason (9.5). Setting versions are dated by instants (`tstzrange`), as user versions are (9.5), and a reading of a setting takes the version in force at the command's start, so an approved change applies to the next sign-in, request or password set (migration 0020). A version taking effect at its decision is written from its preparation's instant and started again at the decision's recording time; one from a later day starts at that day's start under the Organisation's timezone, and is refused when that day is today or earlier, at preparation and again under the locks (`access.starts-in-past`). The version in force at the new one's start ends there, so the setting is never left unset; an Approved version starting at or after it refuses the approval (`access.version-overlaps`), so a change at the decision cannot pass one already scheduled. Decide locks the setting row and the version at step 1 (code-house-rules 8.2; the runtime role holds `UPDATE (id)` on `setting` for it). The preparers are kept in `setting_version_change` (9.1). Preparing writes a `prepare-setting-version` audit record; the approval an `approve-setting-version` record with the value before and after and a permission-change access record; a rejection a `reject-setting-version` record. An Organisation set up before the step required every setting gets the setting's row with its first prepared version. Whether a version's origin fits the environment (code-house-rules 12.14 "Where a value came from") is not checked yet (RR-401).
- A browser is signed in to one Organisation at a time ([deployment.md](../platform/deployment.md) section 3): the one its session cookie names.
- **As built** (`S1-F01-T09`). **Design choice** throughout; no value is chosen.
  - The limits of an office session are the setting `access.office-session-limits`: the idle-lock seconds and the absolute seconds, effective-dated, with no default. While it is not set, sign-in is unavailable and names it, and an existing session counts as not signed in (code-house-rules 12.14). Shared POS sessions get a setting of their own with registered devices (`S1-F12`).
  - Authenticate keeps the limits at each request, in a transaction of its own, before the request's: past the absolute limit the session ends (`session-ended` access record); past the idle limit since its last activity it locks (`session-locked` access record); otherwise the request is its activity. Every authenticated request counts as activity, so a screen does not poll in the background.
  - **The screen's own lock** (product owner, 7 Oct 2026, `S1-F01-T30`). **Design choice.** The web app keeps one timer, started when the session is active and counted from the last request it sent to a route that needs a session, as the server counts, not from pointer or key activity, which the server never sees. When the idle limit in force passes, it shows the same lock as when the server answers `access.session-locked`: the page kept under the lock, each form dropping its secret fields. A tab that was in the background checks at once when it comes back to the front, since its timer may have run late. The timer sends nothing, since a request of its own would keep the session from ever locking. The server stays the real check: it locks at the next request, and the unlock reaches a session it has not yet locked. The limit is read when the session read is made, so a changed limit reaches a screen at its next sign-in or page load.
  - A locked session reaches only the unlock and the sign-out; every other request is refused `not-signed-in` with the code `access.session-locked`, so the web app tells it from an ended session, which gets `access.not-signed-in` (RR-264). The unlock is an attempt to sign in again: it writes a `sign-in` access record, answers a wrong password with the one sign-in refusal, and counts and slows failures as sign-in does (3.1), by the user's login and the source address.
  - Signing out ends the session (`sign-out` access record) and clears the cookie. A user revokes one or all of their own sessions with no permission (3.2); another user's sessions need edit on `access.session`. Resetting another user's credential needs edit on `access.user_credential`. Neither is a prepared record type: each takes effect at once.
  - With no freshness setting, a fresh code counts only for a time step later than any the person's authenticator gave before, so each protected action needs a code of its own.
  - Unsaved screen input is kept on the device in the browser's local storage, under the Organisation code and the user, without its secret and restricted fields, and offered back after the next sign-in of that user.
- **The session read as built** (`S1-F01-T23`). **Design choice.** `GET /api/access/session` answers the signed-in user's Organisation code, identifier, display name, personas held and effective grants (RR-261, RR-281), whether they hold at least one role assignment in force today (`roleAssignmentInForce`, which decides between My work and "No access assigned"; personas.md section 2; DEC-118, RR-260), and the Organisation's timezone in force (`timezone`, an IANA name), in which the web app formats every time it shows (`PRD-MOD-017`; DEC-118, RR-310; code-house-rules 9). It also gives the idle-lock limit in force for the office session (`idleLockSeconds`, a setting value and no secret), from which the screen shows its own lock (3.3 "The screen's own lock"; `S1-F01-T30`); with no office session limits in force no session authenticates, so the read never answers without it. With no timezone in force no session authenticates and sign-in is unavailable naming it (3.1), so the read never answers without one.

### 3.4 Test sign-in on the development environments

`POL-02.17`, `PRD-ACS-017`; DEC-121; `S1-F01-T28`. **Design choice.**

- On `local` and `dev` only, the sign-in screen shows, below the Sign in form, one button per SYNTHETIC person the server's `AOS_DEMO_SIGN_IN` lists (a JSON array of `{organisationCode, login, label}`). One press signs that person in without the password or the authenticator code.
- The server reads the variable at start (code-house-rules 12.14): unset, the test sign-in is off, `GET /api/access/demo-sign-in` lists nobody and `POST /api/access/demo-sign-in` refuses every request. Set while `AOS_ENVIRONMENT` is not `local` or `dev`, or naming an Organisation whose code does not begin `SYN-`, the server refuses to start. So `kdps-test` and production can never have it.
- Only a listed person, exactly by Organisation code and login, in a SYNTHETIC Organisation, and still Active with a current password, as for any sign-in (3.1); the required security settings must be in force, as for any sign-in. An unfinished first sign-in still leads to enrolment and the password change (3.2). Anything else gets the one sign-in refusal.
- Every attempt in a known Organisation writes an access record of kind `demo-sign-in` (numbering-and-audit 5.2), so the sign-in history never shows it as an ordinary sign-in. The session is an ordinary office session (3.3): the same cookie, idle lock and limits.
- The command carries no idempotency key, as sign-in (code-house-rules 12.4), and passes the same-origin check.

## 4. Permissions, roles and role assignments

### 4.1 Permissions

- A permission is one action on one record type, or one field class (`POL-02.03`, `POL-02.04`, `PRD-ACS-008`).
- The actions are view, create, edit, approve, cancel, export and override. A broad label such as All, Full or Manage is expanded into these before it is saved; it is never stored as a label (`POL-02.03`).
- Each module declares its record types, their actions and which of its fields belong to which field class. A new record type or action is added by a reviewed code change, not at run time. **Design choice.**
- The field classes are those the PRD restricts: salary and payroll data, identity documents, bank details, customer contact information, cost, margin, employee photos and location evidence (`PRD-ACS-008`, `PRD-SEC-010`). A field-class permission is view, or view and edit. **Design choice** of the grouping.
- Holding approve gives no authority to approve a valued action without a limit (section 9.2; `POL-02.05`, `POL-02.15`).

### 4.2 Roles

- A role is a named set of permissions, with a code unique in the Organisation and versions (`POL-02.01`; PRD "Words used").
- KDPS starts from eleven editable templates: Owner, Store POS, Warehouse, Brand Manager, Accounts, Admin, Operations, HR, EBO staff, CA and Auditor (`POL-02.01`). A template is a starting set of permissions copied into a new role; its label grants nothing. The access grid in [ui-blueprint.html](../ui/ui-blueprint.html) (3a) is the design starting point for each template's permissions; KDPS confirms them (V-01).
- **Self-service role** (`PRD-ACS-022`, DEC-041, DEC-100). A role that holds self-service permissions holds nothing else, and every assignment of it has own-record scope (5.4). It is not one of the eleven templates.
- A role change after a new Organisation's setup step (9.11, `PRD-ACS-023`) is a new version, prepared and then approved by an authorised person other than the preparer, effective from its start date (`POL-02.07`; module-map 6.2 flow A).

### 4.3 Role assignments

- A role assignment is a user or service identity, a role, a scope and effective dates. It is the only thing that grants access, except a stand-in grant, which gives only the authority it names for a limited time (section 10; `PRD-ACS-018`). A user may hold several assignments, and each applies only inside its own scope (`PRD-ACS-001`–`PRD-ACS-004`, DEC-001).
- Assignments are effective-dated. Versions never overlap, and history is kept (`PRD-ACS-005`, `PRD-MOD-010`, `POL-02.06`). A version starts today or later, as for masters ([structure-and-masters.md](../masters/structure-and-masters.md) 2.2), and none starts on a past date; there is no exception (DEC-105, GC2-7). **Design choice.**
- Which assignments may be in force together (DEC-112, CH-7). Several assignments of one actor may be: of different roles, or of one role over different scopes. Two assignments of the same actor, the same role and the same exact scope never overlap in time; a replacement ends the earlier one from the replacement's start ([code-house-rules.md](../platform/code-house-rules.md) 7.3). Each assignment is checked on its own (7.1 step 3), so the permissions and scopes of separate assignments never combine to widen authority (`PRD-ACS-004`), and no assignment is approved by its preparer (`PRD-ACS-006`). The canonical form of "the same exact scope" and the constraint that enforces it are settled in [code-house-rules.md](../platform/code-house-rules.md) 7.3 (`S1-F01-T11`). Until ending an assignment early exists, a second assignment of the same actor, role and exact scope over overlapping dates is refused when it is prepared and again when it is approved (`access.assignment-overlaps`).
- Every change after a new Organisation's setup step (9.11, `PRD-ACS-023`), including ending an assignment early, is prepared and then approved by an authorised person other than the preparer, and takes effect from its date (`POL-02.07`). So is withdrawing an approved assignment before its start (Scheduled): the withdrawn assignment is never in force (product owner, 6 Oct 2026, RR-202; [code-house-rules.md](../platform/code-house-rules.md) 7.3). Its state name, Withdrawn, and the mechanism are approved (product owner, 6 Oct 2026, CH-11; [design-language.md](../ui/design-language.md) section 7). On `kdps-test`, before policy 2 is Signed, KDPS staff get role assignments prepared from what KDPS tells us and approved like any other change; they are settings of the test setup, not the signed role map of `POL-02.11`, and enable no gated action (DEC-103, DEC-071). Approving them needs a reason list in force there first: the first list, with the reasons KDPS gives, is a setting of the test setup, approved first with a free-text reason (DEC-105, DEC-104; 9.5, GC3-12).
- **A new user's assignments** (product owner, 6 Oct 2026, DEC-116; RR-215). A user's first version and their role assignments may wait for approval together. Deciding an assignment is refused until the user's first version is approved. Rejecting the user's first version withdraws their pending assignments. Withdrawn covers both cases: a request withdrawn before approval and an approved version withdrawn before it takes effect; the record keeps which it was and why (product owner, 6 Oct 2026, `DEC-117`). As built (`S1-F01-T13`): the rejecting decision withdraws each pending assignment in its own transaction; the assignment takes the final decision Withdrawn, its withdrawal document's version records the kind `before-approval` and the decision that caused it, and the assignment's request becomes Withdrawn. The rejected user's temporary password is retired then too (3.2). **Design choice.**
- To cut a person's access online, disable the user (2.1). Disabling is a user change, so it is prepared and then approved by a different authorised person (9.11, DEC-112), and takes effect at the approval, revoking every session of the user in the same transaction. Revoking the user's sessions (3.3) ends them sooner, but bars no new sign-in. Work already delegated to an offline counter ends at its bounded expiry (`PRD-SEC-008`, `PRD-OFF-003`). **Design choice.**

## 5. Scope

### 5.1 Three dimensions, or own records

- A role assignment's scope has three dimensions: legal entity, place and brand (`PRD-ACS-001`, `POL-02.02`). In each it is all members, which includes future members; selected members, which stay fixed; or empty. An assignment that is empty in any dimension grants nothing (`PRD-ACS-005`).
- The one exception is own-record scope, which has no dimensions. Only the self-service role uses it (`PRD-ACS-021`, `PRD-ACS-022`).
- Selected members are checked through the scope contract of `organisation` and `merchandise` when the assignment is saved (module-map section 3, rule 6).

### 5.2 The place tree

- Places form one tree: a Site, the Stores linked to it on a date, and the business units of each Store; warehouse and office units hang directly under their Site ([structure-and-masters.md](../masters/structure-and-masters.md) 3.9; `PRD-ACS-021`).
- A selected Site covers every Store and business unit at it, including ones added later. A selected Store covers every business unit of it, including ones added later (`PRD-ACS-021`, DEC-094, DEC-098). A selected business unit covers only itself (`PRD-ACS-005`).
- From stage 5, when a Store can relocate, a Store-scoped assignment follows the Store and a Site-scoped one covers the Stores linked to the Site on the record's business date ([structure-and-masters.md](../masters/structure-and-masters.md) 3.9). **Design choice.** A business unit stays at its Site, so a Site-scoped assignment covers it through that Site. A relocating Store gets new business units at its new Site, linked to its old ones, and stock moves between them by transfer (DEC-105, GC2-4; [structure-and-masters.md](../masters/structure-and-masters.md) 3.3).

### 5.3 Matching a record

- Each record type declares which scope facts it carries: legal entity, Site, Store, business unit and brand. The owning module passes its record's facts to Authorise (module-map section 3, rule 6). **Design choice.**
- The legal entity comes from the business unit's mapping version that the record uses, never from the Site (`POL-10.01`; [structure-and-masters.md](../masters/structure-and-masters.md) 3.4).
- For each dimension the record carries, the assignment's scope must cover the record's fact. A dimension the record type does not carry is not checked: a brand or a party has no place, so the permission on its record type decides (4.1). **Design choice.**
- A record with several facts in one dimension:
  - Several brands, such as a document with lines of several brands: covered only when every brand is covered, so a brand-limited assignment never acts on another brand's lines. **Design choice.** A stock header row that can cover goods of several brands, such as a movement, a hold or a reservation, records its brand set, and a brand-limited reader sees it only with access to every brand it records; its cost still needs the cost field permission (product owner, 6 Oct 2026, RR-228; [stock-ledger.md](../stock/stock-ledger.md) SL-25 (d)). Its policy calls `access.row_visible_brand_set`, which checks the place, the legal entity and every brand of the set inside one grant ([code-house-rules.md](../platform/code-house-rules.md) 6.2; `DEC-117`).
  - Two places, such as a transfer: the action names the place it acts at, such as dispatch at the source and receipt at the destination. The stage 3 transfer design names it per action. Allowed destinations are shown by name and code only (`PRD-TRF-004`).
- A fact that is Unknown, such as the brand of unidentified goods, is covered only by all-members scope in that dimension. Unknown never matches a selected member (`PRD-MOD-015`). **Design choice.**

### 5.4 Own records

- Each module declares which of its record types have a subject person. Own-record scope covers those records whose subject is the signed-in user (`PRD-ACS-021`, `PRD-ACS-022`, DEC-041). **Design choice.**
- From stage 6 that includes the person's attendance, targets, incentives and payslips, which `hr` links to the user ([personas.md](personas.md) section 4; `PRD-HRM-012`, `PRD-HRM-015`). Staff also see their own employee record, read-only, through self-service. Its restricted fields follow the self-service role's field permissions (DEC-105, GC3-9). Field-class permissions of the self-service role count as self-service permissions and apply only to own records. **Design choice.** which field classes that role grants is KDPS's and stays OPEN (KDPS question 53; stage 6). A salesperson's own attributed sale lines are declared by `pos` in stage 4 ([personas.md](personas.md), P-SLS).
- Own-record scope never covers another person's record, a legal entity, a place or a brand. An assignment scoped by legal entity, place or brand never grants a self-service permission (`PRD-ACS-022`). Maintain refuses a role that mixes self-service permissions with others, and an assignment of the self-service role with any other scope. **Design choice** that enforces `PRD-ACS-022`.

## 6. Restricted fields and encryption

- Restricted fields are salary, identity documents, bank details, customer contact information, cost and margin (`PRD-ACS-008`), and employee photos, location evidence and payroll data (`PRD-SEC-010`).
- A restricted field is shown only when the same assignment that grants the action also grants its field class (`PRD-ACS-004`, `PRD-ACS-008`). Otherwise a screen shows it masked, so the person knows it exists, and exports, read models, live updates and logs leave it out. **Design choice.**
- Bank details, identity documents, salary and payroll data, and authenticator secrets are encrypted in the application before they reach PostgreSQL, with a key per Organisation held outside the database. The database and its backups hold only the encrypted value (`PRD-SEC-006`, `POL-18.02`). **Design choice.** On test hosting the key is an environment secret ([deployment.md](../platform/deployment.md) section 9): `AOS_ORGANISATION_KEYS` maps each Organisation code to its 256-bit key. From it the application derives one subkey per purpose (HKDF-SHA-256: the authenticator secret, restricted values kept in a refused request, the throttling digest), and encrypts with AES-256-GCM, binding each value to its record (`S1-F01-T08`). **Design choice.** Key storage and rotation on production wait for the production hosting design ([deployment.md](../platform/deployment.md) D-1).
- A restricted value never appears in a log, an error message, an unauthorised cache or a live-update event (`PRD-SEC-006`, `PRD-SEC-014`). The counter never receives cost, margin or receipt-origin value (`PRD-OFF-004`).
- The audit record keeps no copy of an encrypted value, and showing or exporting one writes an access record ([numbering-and-audit.md](../platform/numbering-and-audit.md) 4.3 and 5.2).

## 7. Checking an action

### 7.1 The order

Every request and every job step goes through steps 1 to 3, which are steps 1 and 3 of module-map 6.1. A command that takes locks also goes through step 4, which is step 6 of module-map 6.1. Sign-in and a user's actions on their own credentials and sessions (3.1, 3.2) are one exception: they need only step 1 once a session exists. A new Organisation's setup step (9.11) and the platform operator's recovery command (3.2) are the others.

1. **Authenticate.** The session is in force, not locked and not Revoked, and the user is Active (2.1); for a service identity, the identity is enabled and its credential is not revoked (`PRD-INT-001`, `PRD-ACS-017`, `PRD-SEC-008`, `PRD-SEC-018`). A session started with a temporary password, or by a user with no confirmed authenticator, reaches only enrolment and the user's own password change until what 3.2 requires is done; every other request is refused here.
2. **Available.** `configuration` answers whether the operation is available here and now: the capability is on, the policy is Signed, the owning module's configured records are valid, and the activity is granted where one applies (`PRD-SEC-017`, `PRD-LIF-001`; module-map 4.4). Otherwise the request is refused with the blocking reason. This is the stage 1 exit check "an operation whose policy is not configured stays unavailable". Setup and configuration operations (access, structure, masters, book setup, readiness and policy readiness) are not policy-gated, because they are how a policy gets configured: this step asks no Signed policy of them, but steps 1, 3 and 4 and their independent approvals apply in full. Operations that record business effects are gated: posting stock or money, publishing opening data and selling on a device. A synthetic Organisation may record labelled synthetic Signed statuses for tests and demos; `kdps-test` and production never do (product owner, 6 Oct 2026, DEC-116; `PRD-SEC-017`).
3. **Authorise.** Find one role assignment, or for an approval decision one stand-in grant (section 10), that, on the date of the action, grants the action on the record type, covers the record's scope facts (5.3), and grants every restricted field class the action reads or writes (`PRD-ACS-001`, `PRD-ACS-004`, `PRD-INT-001`). Authorise returns the assignment it used; the audit record and any approval decision store it. An assignment empty in any dimension grants nothing, even on a record type that does not carry that dimension (`PRD-ACS-005`). For a route, the request's guard runs it on the route's action and record type when the record type declares no scope fact; otherwise the route declares that its command authorises, and the command runs it with each record's facts; in a list, each row's (`S1-F01-T11`, `S1-F01-T18`; RR-296). A job step runs it for the one action its job kind or consumer declares, and so does a redelivery before it is replayed (RR-273). **Design choice.**
4. **Under the locks.** Check again that the user is Active, or the service identity enabled; repeat step 3 for the same assignment; and make the approval checks of 9.7 (`PRD-INT-003`; stock-ledger 10.4).

- The date of the action is today under the Organisation's timezone, not the record's business date: authority is about who may act now. **Design choice.**
- Authorise reads the assignments and their role versions themselves, never the effective grants: an assignment, and the role version it grants through, authorise only while their dates hold today, so an end that passed refuses the very next request, and a Scheduled assignment authorises from its start, whatever the grants rebuild has done (DEC-120, RR-390). Under the locks (step 4) the same check runs again with the user Active or the service identity enabled.
- A refusal names what is missing: the action, the place, the brand or the field class, and where one assignment nearly covers it, which one ([personas.md](personas.md) section 3; `PRD-UXP-003`). As built (`S1-F01-T11`), `access.not-authorised` carries one item: `permission` (record type and action) when no assignment grants the action; otherwise `scope` (the dimension, or own records) or `field-class`, with the assignment that nearly covers it.
- Reading a file attached as evidence is a route of the access kind `attached-record` (`S1-F06-T05`): the guard Authenticates only, and the command finds the attachment under row-level security and runs step 3 for view on the attached record's own type, with its scope facts and every restricted class the attachment carries ([imports-and-opening-data.md](../platform/imports-and-opening-data.md) section 11). **Design choice.**
- Jobs, imports, live updates, search, reports and AI answers go through the same steps under their own actor (`PRD-SEC-005`; module-map section 3, rule 8).

### 7.2 Row-level security

- Every table of scoped rows carries the row's scope facts as columns and has a PostgreSQL row-level security policy (`PRD-SEC-005`). The runtime role neither owns the tables nor bypasses the policies ([deployment.md](../platform/deployment.md) section 4).
- A row carries the identifiers of its Site, Store and business unit, as they apply. So a grant for a Site, a Store or a unit matches by equality, and SQL never expands the tree. **Design choice.**
- `access` keeps an effective-grant table: for each actor and record type, the places, legal entities and brands its assignments cover, with all members kept as a wildcard. It is derived from the assignments and stand-in grants, and rebuilt when they change (`PRD-ACS-005`, `PRD-ACS-018`). **Design choice.**
- **Dates checked when read** (DEC-120, RR-390; migration 0022). Each grant row belongs to one assignment and one Approved version of its role, and keeps the business days both hold; rows are kept for every pair still holding today or later. `kernel` sets the transaction's business date beside the actor (`aos.business_date`, today under the Organisation's timezone at the command's instant), and `access.row_visible` admits a grant only while its days hold that date; with no date set it admits none. So row-level security follows a start or end date on its day, as Authorise does. The scheduled rebuild (`access.rebuild-grants`) is only a refresh of the rows and a notice of what changed (13.1); it never decides access. Its interval for synthetic and test work is 60 seconds (code-house-rules 12.9).
- `kernel` sets the actor in a transaction-local setting at the start of each transaction, from the Scope for the database operation of `access` (module-map 4.1 and 4.3). Each policy admits a row when one grant of that actor for the row's record type covers the row's facts, or, for a self-service grant, when the row's subject is the actor. With no actor set, every scoped table shows no rows. **Design choice.**
- Row-level security is a backstop. It limits rows to those some assignment of the actor could reach; the exact check is Authorise, one assignment per action (`PRD-ACS-004`). In a list, each row is shown through one assignment that grants view on it.
- Restricted columns are protected by masking and encryption (section 6), not by row-level security.
- Read models carry the same scope columns and policies, so they are read under the reader's own authorisation (module-map section 3, rule 5). **Design choice.**

## 8. Approval rules

- One approval rule per action type that needs approval. Its fixed parts come from the PRD and the policies and are kept in code: whether independent approval is required (`PRD-ACS-006`, `POL-02.07`), the value basis (`PRD-ACS-015`), and what counts as a material change (`POL-02.12`, with narrower rules such as `PRD-TRF-010`). [domain-model.md](../architecture/domain-model.md) section 5 lists them. Independence that a source requires can never be switched off.
- Its configured parts are effective-dated settings with no default: whether bulk approval is allowed (`POL-02.19`), whether phone approval is allowed (`POL-02.22`), and the approve and reject reasons (`POL-02.23`). A change to them is approved by an authorised person other than the preparer (`POL-02.07`); a decision on a change to the reason list gives a free-text reason (9.5, DEC-104).
- Where `PRD-ACS-015` names no basis for an action, the value bases of DEC-105 apply (DM-8; [domain-model.md](../architecture/domain-model.md) section 5): booking approval, the booking's value at cost; damage confirmation, acceptance of excess, wrong or unidentified goods, and each supplier-return step, cost; day-close cash variance, the difference; payroll inputs, the period's net pay; offers, no value limit. **Design choice.** The limits on those bases are KDPS's and stay OPEN (V-02). The mechanism supports a basis or none.

## 9. Approvals

### 9.1 Requesting approval

- The owning module calls Request approval with: the action type; the document and its exact version; the material facts (source, destination, items, quantity, amount and beneficiary, as they apply); the value on its basis, or Unknown; the scope facts; and the preparers (`PRD-ACS-007`, `PRD-ACS-015`).
- The preparers are every user who recorded a change in the version under approval, including the one who submitted it (DEC-105, GC3-1). **Design choice.**
- Refused when the action has no approval rule in force or the operation is unavailable (7.1 step 2).
- One open request per document version and action type. **Design choice.**
- `access` publishes the request to My work through the outbox (module-map section 3, rule 6).

### 9.2 Approval limits

- A limit is set for one action type, either for an approver role within a scope or for a named individual (`POL-02.09`, `POL-02.15`). It states its basis from the action's rule (`PRD-ACS-015`): cost, bill value, documented valuation, the amount paid, the difference of a day-close cash variance or the period's net pay, in paise (DM-8, DEC-105); a quantity with its unit; or a discount percentage where configured. The basis is shown beside the limit (`PRD-ACS-015`, `POL-02.09`).
- A missing limit grants nothing. Unlimited authority is an explicit setting (`POL-02.09`, `POL-02.15`). Authority over Unknown value is a separate explicit setting (`PRD-ACS-016`).
- An individual limit names the user and the assignment it applies to, and replaces the role's limit for that user and action. **Design choice.**
- A limit covers a value when the value on its basis is at most the limit. **Design choice.**
- Limits are effective-dated, and a change is approved by an authorised person other than the preparer (`POL-02.07`). The values are OPEN (V-02; KDPS Owner; stage 1 live approvals).

### 9.3 Who may decide

An eligible approver for a request is a person who, when deciding:

- holds an assignment in force that grants approve on the action's record type and covers the request's scope facts (`PRD-ACS-001`, `PRD-ACS-004`), or holds a stand-in grant that covers the request (section 10);
- has, through that same assignment or stand-in grant, a limit that covers the value on its basis, or explicit authority over Unknown value when the value is Unknown (`PRD-ACS-004`, `POL-02.09`, `PRD-ACS-016`). For PT approval, a missing or disputed proposed acquisition cost blocks value-based approval until it is resolved (`PRD-ACS-016`, `POL-02.09`). An action whose approval has no value ("—") or no value limit (offers, DM-8; [domain-model.md](../architecture/domain-model.md) section 5) needs only the approve permission. For an action whose basis `PRD-ACS-015` does not name, the value basis is the one DEC-105 sets (section 8; [domain-model.md](../architecture/domain-model.md) section 5);
- is not one of the preparers, through any role (`PRD-ACS-006`, `POL-02.08`);
- is a person, never a service identity (2.3).

A higher authority is a different person whose limit covers the action on its value basis (PRD "Words used", DEC-043). Transfers need one (`PRD-TRF-005`).

### 9.4 Routing

- The request is offered to the eligible approvers with the lowest limit that covers its value; explicit unlimited authority comes after every finite limit. If nobody is eligible, it stays Awaiting approval and is never approved automatically (`POL-02.09`, DEC-043). **Design choice** of the order.
- Nobody forwards a request by hand: there is no action to send it to a next approver. It waits for an approver whose limit covers it, and escalates when overdue, as below (product owner, 6 Oct 2026, DEC-116; [design-language.md](../ui/design-language.md) 10.14).
- Eligibility is worked out when My work is read and again at Decide, so a change to assignments, limits or stand-ins applies as soon as it is in force. **Design choice.**
- Overdue requests escalate under a configured rule (`PRD-ACS-010`). An approval gets a due time and an escalation recipient per action type and Site, set like exception routing (12.2) as effective-dated settings (DEC-105, GC3-8). An escalation adds the recipient and keeps the owner (11.3). **Design choice.** No policy names the values, so they are KDPS's and stay OPEN (KDPS question 52; stage 1 live approvals).

### 9.5 Deciding

- The approver approves or rejects, with a reason from the configured list, evidence and a comment (`PRD-ACS-010`, `POL-02.23`). Evidence is stored files attached through `files-imports` from the start (`S1-F06-T05`; product owner, 6 Oct 2026). Until the list is configured, deciding is unavailable, since nothing is on by default. The one exception is a decision on a change to the reason list itself, which gives a free-text reason, so the first list can be approved (DEC-104). On `kdps-test` before policy 2 is Signed, the first reason list, with the reasons KDPS gives (KDPS Owner question 45), is a setting of the test setup, approved first by a different authorised person with a free-text reason; it enables no gated action (DEC-105, DEC-104, DEC-071; GC3-12). Until it is in force, the test role assignments of 4.3 cannot be decided.
- Decide checks 9.3 again, that the request is still open, and that the document's current version is the one requested (`PRD-ACS-007`).
- The decision records the approver, the time, the outcome, the reason, the evidence, the comment, the version decided, the value and its basis, the approved amount where the document states one, and the role assignment and limit version, or the stand-in grant, it relied on ([domain-model.md](../architecture/domain-model.md) 3.2). It is an entry and is never edited.
- Then, depending on the document (module-map 6.2): a master version takes effect in the same transaction (flow A); a document posts in the same transaction; or a large document waits for its job (9.8).
- A rejection returns the document to its preparers. A new version needs a new request.
- **As built for access changes** (`S1-F01-T13`). **Design choice** throughout; no value is chosen.
  - Each access change has an approval rule in code (section 8): user, role, role assignment, withdrawal of an assignment, approve and reject reason, approval rule setting, and, from `S1-F01-T25`, an essential security setting (3.3), each approved through approve on its record type, independently, with no value (DM-8). Preparing a version saves it Awaiting approval and requests its approval in the same transaction (9.1); a separate Draft before submission is not built yet (RR-292).
  - The request keeps the preparers read from the version's change rows when it is requested; Decide refuses anyone among them or among the change rows then (9.3). A request still open on an earlier version of the same document is Superseded by the new one; the earlier version row keeps its decision Awaiting approval, and only its request says Superseded, so code-house-rules 7.3's decisions are unchanged.
  - Deciding is a route of its own kind: Authenticate in the guard, then Authorise for approve on the request's record type in the command, under the locks. Decide locks the authority rows at step 0 and then the request and the document's rows at step 1, one call each (code-house-rules 8.2; RR-325): at step 0 the approver's user row and the role assignment Authorise returned, shared, and the authority rows the decision changes, exclusive: the user's identity row for a user version (with the pending assignments a rejection of the first version withdraws), the assignment for an assignment or its withdrawal. Under the locks it rechecks eligibility, and refuses as stale (`kernel.stale-version`) when another assignment than the one locked is now the one relied on. A preparing command does the same at step 0 for its preparer and the assignment the route's Authorise found (`holdAuthority`). So a decision and the disabling of its approver never pass each other: whichever locks second waits and sees the other's commit. The runtime role holds `UPDATE (id)` on `app_user` and `service_identity` for these locks, which code-house-rules 7.1 allows a locked append-only table; the rows stay append-only. The role joins step 0 (product owner, 7 Oct 2026, DEC-118; RR-360): a command relying on a role assignment also locks, shared, the role the assignment grants, and a decision that makes a role version take effect locks the role exclusively, so a role change and a command relying on that role never pass each other; under the locks the command rechecks the role's version in force (code-house-rules 8.2). **Design choice** of the mechanism. As built (`S1-F01-T22`): the command reads the role of the assignment it relies on and the role's version in force today before the locks, locks the role with the user and the assignment in the same step-0 call (`holdAuthority`, Decide), and refuses as stale (`kernel.stale-version`) when the role's version in force under the locks differs; Decide on a role version locks the role exclusively at step 0, and so does a role version's effect run on its own, such as the setup step's. The runtime role holds `UPDATE (id)` on `role` for these locks (migration 0018). A role version that starts on a later day takes effect when that day comes, read by date as before, with no decision and so no lock (7.2). Job steps hold their service identity's authority the same way (S1-F01 review): the worker's step takes the identity row, the assignment and the role at step 0, shared, through `JobIdentities.hold`, and a role version taking effect while it waits makes the step stale, retried by the job's setting (code-house-rules 12.9).
  - The reason: a reason-list change takes free text only (DEC-104); any other decision takes one reason of the outcome's kind, approve or reject, in force today. While no reason of that kind is in force, deciding is unavailable (`access.no-reason-list-in-force`). So availability is per outcome: the approval panel's read names the outcomes open now and the reason list missing for any other, and shows that one disabled with what it lacks; with no reason of either kind in force, deciding is unavailable as a whole (S1-F01 review). The refusal codes are `access` codes with their kinds (RR-246).
  - A user version takes effect at the moment it is approved (2.1; `PRD-SEC-019`; DEC-118, RR-321). User versions are therefore dated by instants (`tstzrange`), as the Organisation's timezone is (code-house-rules 9), not by business dates: the version in force ends at the decision's recording time and the new one starts there, so a user approved today can be disabled today, and a disabling takes effect, with every session revoked and new sign-in refused, in the decision's transaction. **Design choice.** As built in `S1-F01-T13` they were dated by day and refused a version following one that started the same day (`access.version-overlaps`). As built in `S1-F01-T22`: migration 0018 changes `app_user_version.valid_during` to `tstzrange` in place, keeping its exclusion constraint, its guard trigger and every row, each recorded day becoming the instant it starts under the Organisation's timezone. A version Awaiting approval is written from its preparation's instant and started again at its decision's; the setup step's first users start at the step's start. The version in force is the Approved one with no end: user versions never start after their decision, so it is the latest committed, and a command that waited at step 0 for a disabling sees it, whatever instant it started at. A decision whose recording time is earlier than the start of the version in force, one that began before another decision on the same user but locked after it, is refused as stale (`kernel.stale-version`) and tried again, since a range cannot end before it starts. The access setup screens show a user version's dates as the business days of its instants under the Organisation's timezone, and its state worked out at the instant of the read.
  - Evidence files are not taken yet: they arrive with `S1-F06-T05` (RR-320).

### 9.6 Material change

- A change to a material fact after a request or a decision ends its force. The new version needs a new request (`PRD-ACS-007`, `POL-02.12`). The request that the change ended is shown as Superseded (DEC-105, DM-4; [design-language.md](../ui/design-language.md) section 7).
- The owning module declares, per action type, which of the facts bound by `PRD-ACS-007` and listed in `POL-02.12` are relevant to it. A narrower rule applies only where a source gives one: for a transfer, only an increase in quantity, a change of item or a change of destination is material (`PRD-TRF-010`, DEC-036).
- When a change that is not material makes a new version, the decision stays in force, and the module records that it carried to the new version. **Design choice.**

### 9.7 Recheck under the locks

After the authority rows of step 0, the posting module locks the document and its approval rows (stock-ledger 10.3, step 1) and asks `access` to verify (stock-ledger 10.4):

- the decision is Approved and not yet used (9.8);
- the version being posted is the version decided; or, when a later step of the document posts it, such as a transfer's dispatch, a version the decision carried to (9.6). A queued posting takes only the version its request to post names (9.8, DEC-097);
- the approver is still not one of the preparers (`PRD-ACS-006`);
- the value under the lock, on its basis, is within the limit the decision relied on and within the approved amount. Otherwise posting is refused and the document returns for renewed approval (`PRD-ACS-007`, `POL-02.12`, DEC-066).

The approver's authority is judged when they decide; the recheck uses the limit recorded in the decision. **Design choice.**

### 9.8 Approval of a document a job posts (settles SL-22)

A large document is approved by a click and posted later by a job, one at a time per accounting book (stock-ledger 10.6). DEC-097 settles what happens between the two.

1. **The click.** One transaction records the Approved decision, its audit record, a request to post naming the decision and the version, and the outbox row for the job. It has no stock or money effect.
2. **The job.** It acts as a service identity on behalf of the approver (2.3). It does the slow work first, then one transaction: it locks the document and the decision (stock-ledger 10.3, step 1), rechecks (9.7), where the version posted must be the one the request to post names, posts, and records the use of the decision: this decision authorised this posting. The use commits with the stock and money records; it is the approval evidence of `PRD-INT-004`.
3. **Once.** A decision has at most one use, enforced by a unique key. A replayed job finds the use and does nothing more (`PRD-INT-002`, `PRD-INT-008`).
4. **A failed job.** Nothing is posted. The decision stays Approved and unused, and the document shows the failure (stock-ledger 10.6). A job that still fails after its retries raises an unfinished-operation exception (`PRD-EXC-001`), routed under `POL-02.16`. **Design choice.**
5. **A retry.** Started by an authorised person, or by the job runner under its retry rule (the code house rules; **Design choice**). It uses the same decision only while the document version is unchanged and the value under the lock is within the decision's limit and the approved amount; otherwise the document returns for renewed approval (`PRD-ACS-007`, DEC-066).

### 9.9 Bulk approval

- Only for action types on the configured allowlist (`PRD-ACS-011`, `POL-02.19`). The allowlist is OPEN (alignment report B-9; KDPS Owner, Admin).
- The screen shows the selected items and their total on their shared basis: the sum of the known values and the number of items whose value is Unknown, which are never added as zero. Items on different bases are not totalled together (`PRD-ACS-019`, `PRD-MOD-015`). **Design choice** of how Unknown is shown.
- Each item is its own decision in its own transaction, rechecked for permission, scope, limit, state and independence. An item that fails goes to individual review; the others go on. A batch identity links the decisions (`PRD-ACS-019`, `POL-02.19`). **Design choice.**

- From stage 5 ([phases.md](../../phases.md)). A message carries a link to the request and its exact version. The link is not a credential: opening it needs a signed-in session with the second factor, and the checks of 9.5 apply (`PRD-ACS-012`). A plain reply is never an approval.

### 9.11 Changes to access

- Changes to users (DEC-112), roles, permissions, role assignments, limits, approval rules, allowlists and reasons are prepared, approved by an authorised person other than the preparer, effective-dated, and kept with their history (`POL-02.06`, `POL-02.07`; module-map 6.2 flow A). Stand-in grants follow the same route (DEC-105, GC3-7).
- Each writes an audit record and a permission-change access record ([numbering-and-audit.md](../platform/numbering-and-audit.md) 5.2; `PRD-SEC-007`), when it takes effect; for a user, every approved version does, a change of display name included (RR-214). The audit record of a change that takes effect by a decision, and the decision's own, keep the reason the decision gave, so the record's history says who prepared it, who decided it, why, and which version (numbering-and-audit 4.2 "Reason"; `PRD-ACS-013`; found by `S1-F01-T20`).
- A new Organisation's first Admin and its first approver of access changes are created together in one setup step, recorded and audited under a service identity, because no user of the Organisation can yet approve it. Every later change follows independent approval (`PRD-ACS-023`, DEC-101). The first Admin and the first approver are two different people, since an approver must differ from the preparer (`PRD-ACS-006`). The step runs once, while the Organisation has no user yet; it is the platform's own operation, so no role assignment authorises it, and its audit record names the service identity as the actor. That it runs once, and how it is authorised, are the baseline of DEC-112 below.
- **How it runs.** An authorised platform operator runs the setup step as an operator command that creates and migrates the new Organisation's database; no user of any Organisation can run it (DEC-112, CH-1). It is never an API endpoint. It creates and migrates the database with the migration role, then writes its rows in one transaction as the runtime role under the `setup` service identity, so row-level security and the guards apply to them, and it registers the Organisation code in the directory last ([code-house-rules.md](../platform/code-house-rules.md) 4.3, 5.1). **Design choice**, from the house rules' proposal for CH-1.
- **The request.** The Organisation code; for each of the two users, the login, the display name, the personas held and the temporary password (3.2); and the Organisation's settings, synthetic on `dev`. The temporary passwords are its only secrets. **Design choice.**
- **The request as built** (`S1-F01-T10`). **Design choice.** Besides the fields above, the request names the Organisation's database, by the name the directory keeps ([deployment.md](../platform/deployment.md) section 4), since the code's format is not designed. The settings are: the origin every version records (KDPS, test setup or synthetic; [code-house-rules.md](../platform/code-house-rules.md) 12.14), the timezone (`configuration`'s, code-house-rules 9) and the password rules (3.2), which are required because the step dates every version under the timezone and checks both temporary passwords against the rules, only once it knows it will create the Organisation, so a finished setup is refused as a duplicate before any password is checked, and naming neither user when one fails (spec section 10); and the sign-in throttling (3.1) and the office session limits (3.3). As built in `S1-F01-T10` these two could be left out; under DEC-118 the step requires them too (`S1-F01-T25`): before anything is read or written it refuses a request that leaves out any required security setting, naming each as `settings.<field>`, and the operator command does the same with the request file before asking for the passwords (3.1). The canonical form below is unchanged by this: every request it now fingerprints holds all four. The operator gives the non-secret fields in a request file and types the passwords at the prompt. The step also writes the internal service identities the worker runs as, from the worker's registry, each with a role holding exactly the actions its steps declare and an all-members assignment (2.3; RR-271, RR-290); the `setup` identity itself gets no assignment. A database the directory already names under another code is refused as a conflict, so the step never writes into another Organisation's database (DEC-093).
- **The canonical form, version `setup-fingerprint/1`** (`S1-F01-T10`; RR-211). **Design choice.** Every non-secret field of the request as its schema parses it, the passwords dropped: the code and the database name exactly; each login exactly, its letter case kept, since the step writes the login as given; the personas in the order given, since their order is kept (personas.md section 2); an absent setting absent, never null or a default; as JSON with sorted keys and no insignificant space, hashed with SHA-256.
- **Running it again** (DEC-112). The step reads the directory first, then the Organisation's database, and the two together give its state:

  | Directory | Organisation's database | State | What the run does |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | Lists the code | Any | Finished | Refused as a duplicate before anything else is read: no password is checked and nothing is written |
  | No entry | None; or one with no setup record and no user | Not started, or interrupted before its transaction committed | Creates the database if it is missing, migrates it, writes the transaction, registers the code: created |
  | No entry | A setup record, and the request is identical | Interrupted after its transaction | Migrates the database to the current migrations, since the pre-deploy runner migrates only databases the directory lists ([code-house-rules.md](../platform/code-house-rules.md) 4.3); writes nothing else in it; registers the code: completed |
  | No entry | A setup record, and the request is not identical | Conflicting | Refused as a conflict; nothing is written |
  | No entry | A user but no setup record | Conflicting | Refused as a conflict; nothing is written |

- **Identical** means both: the fingerprint of the request's non-secret fields equals the one the setup record keeps (13.1), and each temporary password in the request verifies against the Argon2 password credential the first run wrote for that login (3.2). When the fingerprints match, the step checks both passwords before it answers; when they differ, it reads no password. When the non-secret fields match but the temporary password of either user does not, the request is refused as a conflict and nothing is written. Every conflict refusal says only that the request conflicts with an earlier run of the step, or that the step's version changed: never which user or field differs, and never a value supplied. **Design choice.**
- **Why the first run's credential still decides.** An interrupted setup has no directory entry, so nobody can sign in to it (3.1): neither temporary password can have been used or changed between the runs, and the credentials the first run wrote are still the ones to verify against. A finished setup is refused before any password is read. **Design choice.**
- **The fingerprint's version.** The setup record keeps, beside the fingerprint, the version of the canonical form it was made with. A rerun made by a step whose canonical form has another version is refused as a conflict, and that refusal says it is a change of version; it never compares fingerprints made two ways. **Design choice.**
- **No secret is kept for the check.** The fingerprint is a SHA-256 hash of a canonical form of the non-secret fields only. No plaintext, hash or other form of a temporary password is kept in the setup record, the fingerprint, the directory, the service log, an error or an audit record (`PRD-SEC-006`, `PRD-SEC-014`); the Argon2 credential of 3.2 is the only verifier. Verifying against it needs no new secret material. A fast hash of a password in the fingerprint could be guessed offline from a copy of the database, a keyed hash would need a key of its own, and either would stay in the append-only setup record after the user had replaced the password; so neither is used. **Design choice.**
- **A refused rerun is kept for investigation** (`PRD-INT-002`). A conflicting or duplicate rerun writes nothing to either database, so the operator command keeps it in the service log: one line with the fingerprint of the refused request's non-secret fields, its canonical-form version and which row of the table above refused it. The line holds no Organisation code, since a conflicting code is not in the directory ([code-house-rules.md](../platform/code-house-rules.md) 12.11), and never a secret or any form of one; how long service logs are kept is OPEN with CH-9. **Design choice.**
- **Correcting a conflict.** The step never resolves a conflict or deletes a database. The operator removes the unregistered database deliberately and runs the step again. **Design choice.**
- **Two runs at once** for one code. The runner migrates one database at a time ([code-house-rules.md](../platform/code-house-rules.md) 4.3). The setup record holds at most one row, and the directory is unique by code. A run that loses the race to create the database, or whose setup-record write or directory entry fails or reaches the lock limit (code-house-rules 5.1, CH-3) because another run holds or wrote it, reads the state again from the start, as in the table; it never takes a failure or a timeout as success. So one run registers the code and the other is refused as a duplicate, or as a conflict if their requests differ. **Design choice.**
- **The first Admin and the first approver** (DEC-112). The first Admin prepares users, roles, role assignments, approval rules, changes to the reason list and new versions of the essential security settings (DEC-120); the first approver inspects them and approves or rejects them; neither decides their own preparation (`PRD-ACS-006`). Each gets the Organisation-wide view of those records and their history that these duties need. Neither gets, from the setup step, any permission to post a business document or to see payroll, bank, customer-contact, cost or margin data; such access comes only through a later role assignment, approved by a different authorised person. Their scope is all members in each dimension until selected members exist. The exact permissions of the two roles (record type, action, field class, scope) are those of the matrix below, derived from the Admin template of the access grid in [ui-blueprint.html](../ui/ui-blueprint.html) (3a) within these duties and limits, and independently reviewed before the setup step is accepted (RR-017). This is a baseline pick: the KDPS Owner confirms the two roles or asks for a change (V-01), and who holds them in a real Organisation is KDPS's (V-04).
- **The two roles' permissions** (baseline of DEC-112; KDPS Owner confirms, V-01). Each user holds one assignment of their role, with all members in legal entity, place and brand; neither role has own-record scope or a self-service permission (5.4). Record types are named here by what they hold; their registry names are fixed when `access` and `audit` declare them (4.1).

  | Record type | First Admin | First approver |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | User: details, personas held, state (Active, Disabled, Ended; disabling revokes the user's sessions at its approval, 2.1; making a Disabled user Active again is a user version like any other), and the first temporary password (3.2) | view, create, edit | view, approve |
  | Role, with its permissions | view, create, edit | view, approve |
  | Role assignment, with its scope; ending one early, and withdrawing a Scheduled one before its start, are edits, prepared by the first Admin and approved by the first approver (4.3; [code-house-rules.md](../platform/code-house-rules.md) 7.3) | view, create, edit | view, approve |
  | Approval rule setting, with bulk and phone allowed (section 8) | view, create, edit | view, approve |
  | Approve and reject reasons (9.5) | view, create, edit | view, approve |
  | Essential security settings: sign-in throttling, password rules, office session limits (3.3; `access.setting`, which takes no create). The first Admin prepares a new version; the first approver approves it; neither holds both (DEC-120, RR-402) | view, edit | view, approve |
| Approval request and approval decision | view | view |
  | History: the audit records of the record types above, and the access records of sign-ins and sessions, second-factor and password events, and permission changes ([numbering-and-audit.md](../platform/numbering-and-audit.md) 4, 5.1) | view | view |
  | Every other record type, including another user's credentials (a reset, 3.2; `access.user_credential`), other users' sessions (`access.session`), approval limits, stand-in grants, service identities, devices, other settings, policy status and every business record | none | none |

  - Field classes: none, so every restricted field stays masked to both (section 6).
  - Actions never granted: cancel, export and override. Approve is never granted to the first Admin, and create and edit never to the first approver, so the first approver can never be a preparer.
  - My work and a user's own credentials and sessions need no permission (3.2, 11.2). An access change has no value, so approving one needs only the approve permission (9.3).
  - **Design choice:** from the Admin template (grid 3a: Setup "Configure; prepare role and rule changes"; Home "Own") come view, create and edit on the records the duties name. ## 10. Stand-ins

- A stand-in grant names the stand-in, the person they stand in for, the actions, scope and limits it gives, and its start and end. It ends by itself at the end time (`PRD-ACS-018`, `POL-02.20`).

## 11. My work

### 11.1 Work items

- A work item belongs to `inbox` and is a projection of the owner's record ([domain-model.md](../architecture/domain-model.md) 3.3). It holds: the kind (task, approval or exception); the owning module, record type, record and version; who may act, as named users or as the owner's rule for who is eligible; the due time; the exposure in paise or Unknown; and the scope facts.
- The owning module publishes, updates and closes its items (module-map 4.8). `exceptions` and higher modules call `inbox` in their own transaction; `access` publishes through the outbox (module-map section 3, rule 6). For a task or an approval, `inbox` sets the due time and escalation recipient from `work_item_routing` when it receives the item (GC3-8, DEC-105). **Design choice.**
- An item is keyed by the owner's record, version and kind, so a replay never makes a second item (`PRD-INT-008`).

### 11.2 The list

- One list per person, with the tasks, exceptions and approvals they may act on, including those a stand-in grant covers (`PRD-ACS-009`, `PRD-ACS-010`).
- Ordered by due time, earliest first, then by exposure, largest first. An Unknown exposure sorts above every known amount at the same due time and never counts as zero (`PRD-ACS-009`, `PRD-MOD-015`). **Design choice** of where Unknown sorts.
- Each item shows its state, its blocking reason and its next action (`PRD-UXP-003`). Opening it opens the owner's record; acting runs the owner's operation with its own checks (module-map 4.8).
- Every signed-in user has My work. `access` filters it, so an item drops out as soon as its reader is no longer eligible. **Design choice.**
- **As built** (`S1-F01-T13`). **Design choice** throughout. Work items are unscoped tables of `inbox`, since My work needs no permission and no grant could admit its reader through row-level security; who may act is a named user, or the eligibility reference `access.approval-request`, which `access` answers at each read with the checks of 9.3. The `inbox` consumers of `access.approval-requested` and `access.approval-decided` run under the `inbox` service identity, holding edit on `inbox.work_item`. An item has no due time while `work_item_routing` has no rows (RR-058); an item with no due time comes after every item with one, and an item with no value, such as an access change, after every Unknown and known exposure; ties keep the order of arrival. A rejection returns the document to its preparers through its Rejected state; a task for them in My work waits for a rule that closes it (RR-323).

### 11.3 Escalation and alerts

- For an exception, a job escalates an open item past its due time under the rule routed for its type and Site (`POL-02.16`); the rules are OPEN (V-03). For a task or an approval, a due time and an escalation recipient are set per action type and Site, like exception routing (12.2), as effective-dated settings (DEC-105, GC3-8). No policy names the values, so they stay OPEN (KDPS question 52; KDPS Owner, Admin; stage 1 live approvals). **Design choice:** an escalation adds the recipient, keeps the original owner and is recorded (`PRD-ACS-010`). The item shows Overdue ([design-language.md](../ui/design-language.md) section 7).
- Before stage 5 nothing is sent (DEC-099). An alert to responsible users about cash gaps, large discounts, missing reports, overdue transit or return deadlines reaches them as a work item or an exception in My work, and on the screen concerned (`PRD-EXC-013`). Which alerts are on, their thresholds and recipients are OPEN (V-70, `POL-02.25`). From stage 5, `notifications` may also send them.
- Live updates carry the item's identifier only; the screen refetches it through `inbox` ([deployment.md](../platform/deployment.md) section 5).

## 12. Exceptions

### 12.1 The record

- An exception holds: a code from a number series ([numbering-and-audit.md](../platform/numbering-and-audit.md)); its type; links to the affected records and their versions; evidence, as stored files attached in `files-imports` from the start (`S1-F06-T05`; product owner, 6 Oct 2026); its exposure in paise or Unknown; its owner; its due time; its status; its Site, and its Store, business unit and brand where they apply (`PRD-EXC-001`; PRD "Words used": Exception).
- Types: shortage, excess, damage, mismatch, transit gap, cash variance, uncertain payment, missing report and unfinished operation (`PRD-EXC-001`), and source conflict (`POL-03.04`). Each module registers the types it raises, the record types they link to, and the resolution check it implements (module-map section 3, rule 6). **Design choice.**
- Raise is keyed by the event that raised it, so a replay never makes a second exception (`PRD-INT-008`).
- It is raised in the transaction that found the problem, or, after a rollback, in a new transaction of its own (module-map 4.13).
- With no open exception-code series, the operation that would raise a numbered exception is unavailable, and the Available check (7.1 step 2) names the missing series. A failed job's record and its diagnostic evidence are still kept and shown in the operations view (module-map 4.1, `PRD-SEC-013`), so nothing is lost (product owner, 6 Oct 2026, DEC-116).

### 12.2 Routing

- A routing rule per type and Site names the owner, the due-time rule and the escalation (`POL-02.16`, `POL-03.05`, DEC-037). Policy 2 routes stock to Operations, money to Accounts and supplier Booking matters to Booking (`POL-02.16`). The real owners, due times and escalation are OPEN (V-03; KDPS Owner, Admin).
- The owner is a named user, or a role within the Site's scope. For a role, the exception appears in the My work of each holder until one takes it. **Design choice.**
- An operation whose exceptions have no routing configured stays unavailable (`POL-02.16`).
- Routing rules are effective-dated settings in `exceptions`, kept with their history (`PRD-MOD-010`).

### 12.3 Lifecycle

- **Raised.** Its first state is Unresolved (DEC-105, DM-4; [design-language.md](../ui/design-language.md) section 7).
- While open, it can be reassigned, commented on, given evidence and escalated (`POL-03.05`).
- **Resolved** when the permitted correction, return, reversal or reconciliation is recorded in its owning module (`PRD-EXC-002`).
- **Closed** only after the owning module's resolution check verifies the linked business outcome (`PRD-EXC-002`) and any required approval is in force (`POL-03.05`). The required approval is the approval of the linked correction in its owning module; a type that needs an approval of its own names it in its stage design. **Design choice.**
- **Overdue** when past its due time. **Reopened** when the problem comes back. Repeated, reopened and unresolved cases are kept (`PRD-EXC-003`).
- An exception settles nothing: closing it changes no stock, money or saleability (`PRD-EXC-003`, `POL-03.05`).
- A new exception of the same type on the same linked record links to the earlier one, so recurring issues show (`PRD-EXC-004`). **Design choice.**
- Read model: open exceptions by Store, brand and type, with exposure and repeats; Unknown exposure is counted apart, never as zero (`PRD-EXC-004`, `PRD-MOD-015`).
